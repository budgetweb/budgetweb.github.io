Planning a Small Business Website in Dubai: A Practical Checklist Before You Hire a Designer

Most small business owners in Dubai do not fail at their website because they picked the wrong designer. They fail because they started without a plan. A designer can only build what you can describe, and a vague brief ("something modern, like our competitor") usually leads to extra revision rounds, a blown budget and a site that still does not bring in enquiries.

This checklist walks through the decisions worth making before you contact anyone. It applies whether you run a clinic in Al Barsha, a trading company in Deira or a home-based catering service in Mirdif.

1. Decide what the website is actually for

Write down the one action you most want a visitor to take. For a restaurant it might be a table booking; for a B2B supplier it is a quote request; for a salon it is a WhatsApp message. Everything on the site should push towards that action.

A useful test: if a visitor lands on your home page from a phone, can they complete that action within two taps? If not, the structure needs work before the visuals do.

2. Know your audience and their languages

Dubai is a multilingual market. Before you budget, decide whether you need:

  • English only
  • English and Arabic (with proper right-to-left layouts, not just translated text)
  • Additional languages such as Russian, Hindi, Urdu or Chinese for specific customer groups

Arabic support affects layout, fonts and testing time, so mention it at the start rather than adding it later. Retrofitting a right-to-left version onto a finished English site is one of the most common causes of overruns.

3. Map out your pages

You do not need a 40-page site to look credible. A typical small business site in the UAE works well with:

  1. Home
  2. About (who you are, your trade licence emirate, your team)
  3. Services or products, ideally one page per main service
  4. Portfolio, case studies or reviews
  5. Contact, with a map, phone, WhatsApp and a short form
  6. Privacy policy

One page per service matters for search. Someone searching for "AC repair in JLT" will be matched far more easily to a page about that exact service than to a single page listing ten things.

4. Gather your content early

Content is the most common bottleneck. Designers wait for text and photos, and launch dates slip. Before your project starts, collect:

  • Your logo in vector format (AI, SVG or EPS)
  • Real photos of your premises, team and work (stock photos are fine for backgrounds, but customers trust real faces)
  • A short description of each service: what it is, who it is for, what it costs or how pricing works
  • Customer testimonials you have permission to publish
  • Your trade name exactly as it appears on your licence, plus address and opening hours

5. Think mobile first

In the UAE, a large share of browsing happens on smartphones, often over WhatsApp links and social media. Ask any designer you speak to how they test on real phones, how fast the pages load on mobile data, and whether buttons are large enough to tap comfortably. A beautiful desktop design that is awkward on a phone will lose customers quietly.

6. Plan for search and local visibility

A website is only useful if people find it. Basic search hygiene should be included in any build:

  • A unique title and meta description for each page
  • Clean, readable URLs
  • Fast loading images (compressed, correctly sized)
  • Your business name, address and phone written the same way everywhere
  • A verified Google Business Profile linked to the site

Ask whether the designer sets these up or whether you need a separate SEO provider.

7. Set a realistic budget and timeline

Prices for web design in Dubai vary widely, from template sites built in a week to custom platforms that take months. When comparing quotes, check what is included: hosting, domain, SSL certificate, number of revision rounds, content writing, Arabic translation, training and post-launch support. A cheaper quote that excludes half of these can end up costing more.

If you want a clear starting point, providers that specialise in web design Dubai projects for small companies usually publish fixed packages, which makes comparison easier than open-ended hourly estimates.

8. Ask who owns what

Before you sign, confirm in writing that you will own the domain, the website files and any accounts (hosting, analytics, Google Business Profile). Many business owners discover years later that their domain is registered in a former freelancer's name. Ownership should always sit with your company.

9. Plan life after launch

A website is not finished on launch day. Decide who will:

  • Update offers, prices and opening hours
  • Apply security and software updates
  • Back up the site
  • Check enquiries arrive in the right inbox

If you plan to manage updates yourself, ask for a short training session and a simple content management system.

Final thoughts

The best websites for small businesses are rarely the flashiest. They are clear about what the business does, easy to use on a phone, quick to load and simple to contact. Spend an afternoon working through this checklist and you will walk into every designer conversation with a brief that saves time, money and frustration — and you will end up with a site that actually works for your business.
